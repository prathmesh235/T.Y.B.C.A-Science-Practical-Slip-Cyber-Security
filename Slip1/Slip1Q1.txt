THE CIA TRIAD IN INFORMATION SECURITY
Q. Explain the CIA triad (Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability) with suitable examples. [10 Marks]

INTRODUCTION
The CIA triad is the core model of information security. It has three goals: Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability. Every security control is designed to protect at least one of them.

1. CONFIDENTIALITY
Definition: Information is accessible only to authorised persons and hidden from everyone else.
Controls: Encryption, passwords, multi-factor authentication, access control.
Example: Bank account details and ATM PIN are visible only to the account holder; online banking uses HTTPS (encryption).

2. INTEGRITY
Definition: Data remains accurate and unaltered, except by authorised users.
Controls: Hashing (SHA-256), digital signatures, checksums, audit logs, backups.
Example: If an attacker changes a transfer of Rs. 1,000 to Rs. 10,000 in transit, integrity is lost. Students' marks must not be changed without permission.

3. AVAILABILITY
Definition: Systems and data are accessible to authorised users whenever needed.
Controls: Backups, redundancy (RAID), failover, load balancers, anti-DDoS tools, UPS.
Example: A shopping website must work during a sale; a DDoS attack that brings it down breaks availability. ATMs and hospital servers run 24x7.

SUMMARY
Principle         | Protects Against           | Key Technique
------------------|----------------------------|---------------------------
Confidentiality   | Unauthorised disclosure    | Encryption, access control
Integrity         | Unauthorised modification  | Hashing, digital signature
Availability      | Downtime / DoS             | Backup, redundancy

CONCLUSION
Confidentiality keeps data secret, Integrity keeps it correct, and Availability keeps it accessible. A good security system must balance all three.
